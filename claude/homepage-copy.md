# Openinvite: Homepage Copy — SOURCE OF RECORD (current as of 23 Aug 2026)

Copy rules observed: US English, USD, no em dashes, no emojis, simple never means basic, never lead with the invite, no security jargon couple facing, no "just" or "simply" about the product, timeline scoped progress only. Duration rule: the hero says nothing about duration; the window appears in section 6. Never "forever", "for life", "yours to keep".

How to read: copy is in blockquotes, ready to build. Everything else is annotation for the builder.

## Section 1: Hero

> **Every morning, one page tells you where you stand.**
>
> Openinvite holds the guest list, the budget, the seating, the vendors and the schedule, then answers the only question that matters: what needs you today.
>
> [Start planning] [See how it works]

Build notes: photography forward, flush left, per the existing design language. No duration claim, no price in the hero. The secondary CTA scrolls to section 2 rather than opening anything.

## Section 2: The daily page, demonstrated

> **This is the whole idea.**
>
> Forty one guests have not replied.
> The florist deposit is due Friday.
> Nothing else needs you today.
>
> That last line is the one people tell us about.

Build notes: this should be the real product surface, not an illustration. Realistic wedding data, never lorem ipsum. If a screenshot cannot carry it yet, set the three lines as type and let the photography do the rest.

## Section 3: What is actually in it

> **Everything in one place, which is the only reason the daily page can be right.**
>
> Guest list. Budget. Seating. Vendors. Schedule. To-dos.
>
> They are not six tools that happen to share a login. They are one system, which is why Openinvite can tell you that the florist deposit matters this week and the seating chart does not.
>
> Ava, your assistant, knows your date, your guest count and which vendors you have not heard back from. Ask Ava anything. The answers are about your wedding, not about weddings.

Build notes: the module names are a single line, not six cards with icons. Cards make it a feature list. The paragraph underneath is the actual argument. Ava naming rule: Ava is "Ava", never "she", in anything user-facing — repeat the name or recast the sentence. Ava is in Pro by owner decision; do not caveat Ava's tier here.

## Section 4: The anti-generic promise

> **Advice about your wedding, not the average one.**
>
> Every planning tool ships a checklist. Most of them are written for a wedding nobody is actually having, which is why couples say the dates are off and the suggestions are noise.
>
> Openinvite reads your real wedding. Your date. Your guest count. Your outstanding vendors. Your budget as it actually stands this week.
>
> If it ever tells you something that is not true for you, that is a bug, and we want to hear about it.

Build notes: the last line is a real commitment. Do not ship it unless there is somewhere for that feedback to land.

## Section 5: Calm, and why it stays calm

> **Nothing in here is trying to sell you anything.**
>
> No vendor ads. No sponsored suggestions. No shop of ours quietly winning every recommendation. Nobody pays us to appear in front of you, because you already paid us, once, and that is the entire business model.
>
> Your guest list is yours. We will never sell it, rent it, or hand it to vendors. Your guests never make an account: they tap a link and reply. And your wedding site is not a public page: we ask every crawler, including the AI ones, to stay out, and we tell them twice. We ask for as little as we can, we protect what we hold, and if you leave you take everything with you.
>
> There is a whole industry that buys and sells the contact details of engaged couples. One list broker names its sources as, in its own words, "bridal & wedding websites, wedding surveys, bridal magazine subscriptions, honeymoon travel information requests, engagement registries, wedding planning resource sites."
>
> That is why people make a second email address before they start planning. You should not have to.
>
> [Read exactly what we collect]

Build notes: state it once, calmly. This section must never become a lecture and must never run longer than what is written above. The link points at the existing privacy policy route for now, with a TODO naming the trust page as the eventual target.

Build notes: this is where the design budget goes — the most striking thing on the page and the least sold. No CTA of its own. Below the fold, never the subject of a CTA. "The grandma test" appears twice on this page (here and section 8) and that is the ceiling.

## Section 8: Questions people actually ask

Six only. Any longer and it reads defensive.

> **Pay once? What is the catch.**
> There isn't one, and the honest answer is the window: one payment, 24 months of access. Nothing inside is metered. No premium tier of a feature you already have, no charge to publish, no add-on that appears the moment the tool becomes useful.
>
> **Why would I pay when the big platforms are free?**
> They are genuinely free, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. They are free because the couple is what is being sold. That is a fair trade if you know you are making it. Ours is the other deal: you pay us once and we answer to you.
>
> **Will my grandmother be able to use it?**
> Your guests never make an account. They tap a link and reply. Couples call this the grandma test, and it is a fair one.
>
> **I already have a registry somewhere else.**
> Link it. We do not push our own shop, because we do not have one. And on gifts: we do not take a percentage of your gifts. We add nothing and take nothing.
>
> **Can I trust a new company with my guest list?**
> That is the right question to ask, and it is why we publish exactly what we collect and what we do with it, in plain words, before you sign up.
>
> **Can I get my data out?**
> All of it, whenever you want, including after the wedding. Reading your wedding back stays free too. There is no archive fee.

Build notes: single CTA. No newsletter capture competing with it. No second button.

## Page-level notes for the build

Order is the argument: promise, proof, system, credibility, trust, price, bonus, objections, ask. Moving the invite up breaks the positioning; moving pricing down means the completeness claim arrives after the reader has decided we are expensive. Section 2 must show the real product — a stock illustration under a functional promise reads as a promise we cannot keep. No logo wall. No testimonial section until there are real beta couples (then it goes between 4 and 5). No countdown, no urgency mechanic, no percentage indicator anywhere.
